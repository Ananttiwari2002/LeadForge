Architecture / Responsible delivery

Feasible begins
with clear boundaries.

The current deliverable is a static, framework-free frontend. A real lead research product and a real HR platform would be separate services with different data, users, risk controls, and integration needs.

Future system boundary

Keep the interface
honest about what it knows.

The frontend should consume explicit, permission-checked services rather than quietly collecting data from browsers or blending unrelated project tracks.

01 / ExperienceAccessible web interface, clear source and status labels, user-controlled review.
02 / Application APIAuthenticated endpoints, validation, authorization, audit events, and rate controls.
03 / Domain servicesSeparate lead qualification and HR workflow services with distinct data models.
04 / ConnectorsOnly approved data providers and HR systems, subject to licensing and integration agreements.
  1. RequestUser defines an allowed task and scope.
  2. AuthorizeCheck identity, role, consent, and policy.
  3. RetrieveUse approved sources or connected systems.
  4. ValidateNormalize data and keep its provenance.
  5. PresentShow evidence, confidence, and uncertainty.
  6. ReviewHuman confirms, corrects, or rejects.

Possible future API seams: organization search, source evidence, qualification criteria, review decisions, HR workflow requests, and aggregate reporting. These are design boundaries, not existing endpoints.

Feasibility and responsibility

Trust is part of
the implementation.

Each project track handles different information. They should not share records, permissions, or inferred data just because they appear in one showcase.

Sources and licensing

Confirm provider terms, permitted access, attribution, reuse, and regional restrictions before any public-source collection. No scraping is performed here.

Privacy and retention

Minimize data and define purpose, retention, deletion, access, and notice. HR records can be sensitive; do not feed them into lead intelligence or general AI tools.

Accuracy and freshness

Store provenance and timestamps, detect duplicates, show gaps, and give people a correction path. A match score is not a verified fact.

Bias and human review

Test qualification criteria for uneven effects. Do not automate employment decisions; keep consequential choices accountable to authorized people.

Security and access

Plan least-privilege roles, strong authentication, encryption, audit logs, secure development, incident response, and periodic access review.

Integration constraints

Confirm APIs, data ownership, update frequency, regional availability, contract limits, and vendor-specific permissions before promising interoperability.

Operational quality

Define monitoring, failure states, support ownership, accessible design, recovery, and clear stale-data handling before production.

Legal and governance review

Obtain qualified review for applicable privacy, employment, marketing, accessibility, and records obligations before a real launch.

Indicative roadmap

Prove the workflow
before adding automation.

Phase 01 / Learn

Validate the problem

Complete the HR research and lead-user discovery separately. Review source rules, define user tasks, and agree on success measures.

  • Participant and data safeguards
  • Workflow prototypes
  • Prioritized requirements
Phase 02 / Prove

Test a narrow slice

Build a controlled prototype with approved test data. Validate permissions, provenance, accessibility, correction, and failure states.

  • No high-impact automation
  • Limited, documented scope
  • Security and usability review
Phase 03 / Operate

Expand with evidence

Only after approval, add integrations and operational support in small increments with ongoing monitoring and user feedback.

  • Vendor and legal approvals
  • Retention and audit controls
  • Measure, review, and iterate
